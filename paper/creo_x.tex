\title{Reusing the repair displacement is not a search signal:\\
a multi-geometry ablation and a shifted-optimum control}
\author{Alan Immanuel Benjamin Vallavaraj}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A recurring idea in constrained and bounded optimisation is that the correction a repair
operator applies to an infeasible candidate is informative: it points away from the
violated region, and so reusing it might guide the search. We test that idea as an
algorithm component rather than as an algorithm. A single term $\beta\,\Delta r$ is added
to the update rule of eight population-based optimisers, where $\Delta r$ is the
displacement the repair operator applied to that individual at its last evaluation. At
$\beta=0$ the arm is stream-identical to the unmodified host, so the comparison is a
within-host ablation in which a matched pair differs by one scalar and nothing else. We
run 29{,}760 matched runs over twelve nonlinearly constrained problems with five repair
operators spanning distinct displacement geometries, and 46{,}080 matched runs of a
shifted-optimum control in which each landscape appears twice, differing only in whether
its optimum sits at the domain centre. Three results follow. First, the term is net
harmful: for every one of the five operators it is significantly worse in more
host-by-problem cells than it is better, and all eight hosts are net harmed. Second, the
apparent benefit of the radial-shrinkage operator, the geometry used by a widely adopted
benchmark protocol, is a centre-seeking artefact: it helps when the optimum lies at the
domain centre and hurts when the same landscape is translated away from it, with a median
centre advantage of $+0.368$ in $\log_{10}$ gap ratio at $D=30$. Third, the natural
explanation that a displacement helps in proportion to the geometric information it
carries is falsified: the ellipsoid-projection operator, whose displacement is a true
surface normal with $D$ degrees of freedom, is the most harmful of the five. We conclude
that displacement reuse is not a usable search signal on static problems under any
geometry we tested, and that benchmark results for this family of mechanism on
centre-biased suites should be read as confounded until a shifted-optimum control is
reported alongside them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The optimisation literature has accumulated a large number of algorithms whose novelty is
metaphorical rather than structural, and a sustained critique has argued that the unit of
analysis should be the \emph{component} rather than the named
algorithm~\citep{sorensen2015,camacho2022}. A component contribution is specified as a
term in an update rule, evaluated by ablation inside hosts that are otherwise unchanged,
and reported with the conditions under which it helps and the conditions under which it
does not.

This paper applies that standard to one specific idea. When a candidate solution violates
a constraint or leaves the search domain, a repair operator moves it to an admissible
point. The vector between the proposed point and the repaired point, which we write
$\Delta r$, is ordinarily discarded. It is tempting to suppose that it is informative:
geometrically it points out of the violated region, so a search that remembers it might
avoid re-proposing the same violation, or might infer something about where the
admissible region lies. Mechanisms of this kind recur in the constrained-optimisation
literature under various names.

We test the idea directly, and the answer is negative in a way that is more informative
than a simple absence of effect. The mechanism is not merely neutral; it is harmful, and
the one setting in which a benefit has been reported for an operator of this family turns
out to be explained by a property of the benchmark rather than by the mechanism.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A component specification of displacement reuse with an exact zero-term baseline:
  at $\beta = 0$ the arm consumes no random numbers and is stream-identical to the
  unmodified host, verified for all eight hosts by an automated test.
\item A multi-geometry ablation over eight hosts, five repair operators and twelve
  constrained problems (29{,}760 matched runs), showing the term is net harmful under
  every geometry tested, including a null operator whose displacement is uninformative
  by construction.
\item A shifted-optimum control (46{,}080 matched runs) that measures, rather than
  argues, the centre-pull confound: the same landscape is optimised twice, differing only
  in whether the optimum sits at the domain centre.
\item A falsification of the obvious mechanistic explanation. If displacement reuse
  worked because the displacement carries geometric information, the operator carrying
  the most information would help the most. It harms the most.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The component}

Let a host algorithm propose, for individual $i$, a candidate by its own update rule
$\mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}$. The component adds exactly one term:
\begin{equation}
  x_i \;=\; \mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}(\cdot) \;+\; \beta\,\Delta r_i ,
  \qquad
  \Delta r_i \;=\; x^{\text{used}}_i - x^{\text{stored}}_i ,
  \label{eq:component}
\end{equation}
where $x^{\text{stored}}_i$ is the point held in the population and $x^{\text{used}}_i$ is
the point actually evaluated after repair. For DE/rand/1/bin the complete rule reads
\begin{equation}
  x_i \;=\; x_{r_1} + F\,(x_{r_2} - x_{r_3}) \;+\; \beta\,\Delta r_i ,
\end{equation}
which differs from the canonical rule~\citep{storn1997} by the final term alone, so a
reader can diff the two in one line.

Two properties make this an ablation rather than a comparison of algorithms. First, the
term is additive and draws no random numbers, so setting $\beta = 0$ recovers the
unmodified host \emph{exactly}, including its random stream; this is asserted for all
eight hosts by a test that forces $\Delta r \equiv 0$ and requires bit-identical
trajectories. Second, the repair operator is a property of the environment and is applied
identically in both arms, so a matched pair of runs differs by the value of $\beta$ and
by nothing else. We hold $\beta = 0.90$ throughout and do not tune it; unbalanced tuning
effort between a proposed component and its baseline is a known failure mode, and leaving
the coefficient fixed is the conservative choice in a study whose finding is negative.

\section{Repair operators}

The central question is whether the usefulness of $\Delta r$ depends on the
\emph{geometry} of the operator that produced it. Five operators are therefore used,
spanning distinct displacement geometries, each parameterised on the search box:

\begin{description}
\item[Radial shrinkage.] Contracts the candidate towards the domain centre outside a soft
  region. Its displacement has one degree of freedom, its magnitude, and always points at
  the centre. This is the geometry used by the preferred-region protocol of a widely
  adopted benchmark suite.
\item[Box projection.] Componentwise projection onto a soft box. The displacement is
  axis-aligned and encodes only which coordinates were violated.
\item[Ellipsoid projection.] Projection onto a randomly oriented ellipsoid, fixed per
  problem so the geometry is part of the problem rather than part of the run. The
  displacement is a true surface normal with $D$ degrees of freedom, and is therefore the
  most geometrically informative operator in the battery.
\item[Rate limit.] Bounds each coordinate's change from the individual's predecessor, so
  the displacement is exactly the component of the requested step that the limiter
  refused. Of the five, this is the operator whose displacement most directly encodes a
  direction the search \emph{wanted} to move.
\item[Random resample.] A null control: a violating candidate is replaced by a fresh
  uniform sample, so the displacement is uninformative by construction. Any benefit here
  is attributable to perturbation magnitude alone, not to guidance.
\end{description}

The rate limit requires a predecessor. We use the incumbent each candidate is generated
from: the target vector for the differential-evolution variants and for the grey-wolf and
$\varepsilon$-constrained hosts, the child's own parent for the genetic algorithm, and the
particle's current position for particle swarm. CMA-ES has no per-individual predecessor,
so we use the distribution mean; we flag this as an analogy rather than a true
predecessor. The initial population is left unrepaired, since sampling has no
predecessor.

\section{Hosts and problems}

Eight hosts span algorithmic-component space rather than metaphor space: DE/rand/1/bin
and DE/current-to-$p$best/1~\citep{storn1997}, L-SHADE~\citep{tanabe2014},
SPSO-2011~\citep{zambrano2013}, a real-coded genetic algorithm with simulated binary
crossover and polynomial mutation, the Grey Wolf Optimiser~\citep{mirjalili2014},
$\varepsilon$-constrained differential evolution~\citep{takahama2009}, and
CMA-ES~\citep{hansen2001}. The Grey Wolf Optimiser is included because it is widely used
in the applied literature, with the explicit acknowledgement that its update rule has been
shown to be a reiteration of particle-swarm ideas rather than a novel
mechanism~\citep{camacho2022}; it appears here as a host, not as a contribution.
Selection is feasibility-aware in every host, following Deb's rules~\citep{deb2000}, so
that feasibility handling is identical across arms.

The constrained campaign uses twelve nonlinearly constrained problems from the standard
G-suite together with two engineering design problems, as implemented in
\texttt{pymoo}~\citep{blank2020}. The shifted-optimum control uses the 24 BBOB
functions~\citep{hansen2021} through the \texttt{IOHexperimenter}
interface~\citep{denobel2023}. All runs use a budget of $2\times10^4$ function
evaluations.

\section{Experimental design}

\paragraph{Constrained campaign.} Eight hosts $\times$ five operators $\times$ twelve
problems $\times$ 31 paired seeds $\times$ two arms gives 29{,}760 runs. The repair
operator acts on the search domain; the problems' own nonlinear constraints are handled
by feasibility-aware selection, identically in both arms.

\paragraph{Shifted-optimum control.} This is the decisive test of the centre-pull
confound. Each BBOB function, at instances 1--5 and $D \in \{10,30\}$, is run in two
variants that differ \emph{only} in where the optimum sits: \emph{translated}, $f(x)$,
with the optimum at $x^\star$ away from the domain centre, and \emph{centred},
$f(x+x^\star)$, the same landscape with the optimum at the origin, which is the centre of
the box. Three operators $\times$ two seeds $\times$ two arms gives 46{,}080 runs. An
operator whose displacement points at the domain centre must help in the centred variant
and not in the translated one. If it does, a benefit reported on a suite whose optima are
biased towards the centre is a centre-seeking artefact rather than repair
guidance~\citep{kudela2022}.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Arms are paired exactly on host, operator, problem, dimension and
seed. On the constrained suite, runs are ordered by the feasibility-aware key, matching
the selection rule used inside the hosts; the objective is compared by Wilcoxon
signed-rank over the 31 seeds within each host-by-operator-by-problem cell, restricted to
runs feasible in both arms, and cells with fewer than six such pairs are reported as
untested rather than silently dropped. On the shifted-optimum control the paired quantity
is $\log_{10}$ of the ratio of optimality gaps, positive favouring the component, and the
confound is tested by comparing the centred and translated improvements for the same
landscape, which are themselves a matched pair. All $p$-values are Holm-corrected within
their family~\citep{holm1979}.

Two observations bear on the mechanism. The null operator, whose displacement is a fresh
uniform sample and therefore carries no guidance at all, is not the worst of the five: it
is better than radial shrinkage and ellipsoid projection on significant-cell counts and
has by far the most ties. And the ellipsoid operator, the most geometrically informative,
fires on $99.7\%$ of candidates and is the most harmful. Firing frequency does not
explain the ordering either: the rate-limit operator fires on $72.1\%$ of candidates and
is the mildest.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What is falsified.} The intuition motivating displacement reuse is that
$\Delta r$ carries directional information about the admissible region. If that were the
operative mechanism, benefit should increase with the information content of the
displacement. It does not. Ordering the operators by degrees of freedom in their
displacement gives radial shrinkage (one, a magnitude), box projection (axis-aligned,
sparse), and ellipsoid projection ($D$, a true surface normal); the ranking by harm runs
in the opposite direction to the ranking by information. A null operator whose
displacement is pure noise outperforms two of the three informative ones. Whatever the
term is doing, it is not conveying constraint geometry to the search.

\paragraph{What the benchmark result was.} Where a benefit for a radial-shrinkage-style
operator has been reported on a standard suite, the shifted-optimum control explains it
without reference to repair at all. The displacement points at the domain centre; on a
suite whose optima are disproportionately near the centre, a centre pull is a free gain,
and the same pull is a penalty once the optimum is moved. This stacks with the known
centre-bias of several widely used benchmark suites~\citep{kudela2022}: a mechanism that
happens to bias search towards the origin will appear to work, and will stop working on
any instance set that does not share that bias. We would suggest that any mechanism whose
displacement has a systematic direction in the search box should report a shifted-optimum
control as a matter of course.

\paragraph{What remains open.} This study tests static problems: the objective does not
move during a run. The intuition behind displacement reuse is arguably better suited to a
\emph{tracking} setting, where a controller repeatedly proposes a step, a rate limiter
refuses part of it, and the target itself moves between iterations. There the refused
component is a statement about a constraint the controller is currently fighting, rather
than about a fixed feasible set it is merely bumping into. Nothing in this paper supports
or refutes that case, and we state it as a conjecture rather than a result. It is also the
only setting we can presently see in which the mechanism's premise is not already excluded
by the evidence reported here.

\section{Threats to validity}

The coefficient $\beta$ is fixed at $0.90$ and untuned; a tuned coefficient might reduce
the harm, though it is hard to see how tuning recovers a benefit from a term whose
informative variant is the most harmful. A single evaluation budget is used, and the
dimensional trend visible in the shifted-optimum control suggests budget and dimension
interact. The predecessor mapping required by the rate-limit operator is a design choice,
and the CMA-ES mapping in particular is an analogy; a different mapping might change that
operator's behaviour, although it is already the mildest of the five. A substantial
number of constrained cells are untested because too few seed pairs were feasible in both
arms, between 21 and 47 of 96 depending on operator, so the constrained result speaks to
the problems where feasibility was attainable within budget rather than to the suite as a
whole. Finally, seven of the eight hosts reproduce their repair fire rates bit-identically
across the two platforms we ran on, but CMA-ES does not, because the reference
implementation differs in version; that arm should be reproduced only against a pinned
version.

\section{Reproducibility}

Code, the submission script, the per-cell results for all 480 cells and the aggregate
tables reported here are available at
\url{https://github.com/alanvallavaraj/CREO-X}. The campaigns are gated: an equivalence
test asserting that $\beta=0$ reproduces the unmodified host, and a liveness check
asserting that every operator actually fires on every host, both run before any compute.
Both campaigns assert full row count and arm coverage before writing output. The
aggregate tables reported here recompute exactly from the committed per-cell table.
