\begin{abstract}
A recurring idea in constrained and bounded optimisation is that the correction a repair
operator applies to an infeasible candidate is informative: it points away from the
violated region, and so reusing it might guide the search. We test that idea as an
algorithm component rather than as an algorithm. The component, which we call CREO, for
Continuous Repair Evolutionary Optimiser, is a single term $\beta\,\Delta r$ added to the
update rule of eight population-based optimisers, where $\Delta r$ is the displacement the
repair operator applied to that individual at its last evaluation. At $\beta=0$ the arm is
stream-identical to the unmodified host, so every comparison we report is a within-host
ablation in which a matched pair differs by one scalar and nothing else.

Our principal result is positive and precisely located. In a deployed rate-constrained
set-point tracking loop the term is a usable control signal: holding the remaining
coefficients fixed, a sweep of $\beta$ against the stream-identical zero-term baseline
produces a dose-response strictly monotone in distance AUC, improving by $2.906\%$ at the
deployed $\beta=0.90$ and by $5.749\%$ at $\beta=1.50$, with $\beta$ alone accounting for
$78\%$ of the configuration's advantage over its bare host. The effect is not peculiar to
one host: every one of the six hosts tested improves, over $67{,}200$ matched pairs each,
with relative gains from $3.136\%$ to $21.306\%$ and Holm-corrected $p$ below
double-precision resolution throughout, and the strongest configuration attains a mean
rank of $1.000$ across twenty-one stress profiles, placing first in every one. The gain is
largest where the host is weakest, with Spearman $+0.829$ between host distance AUC and
relative gain, which is the signature of a component rather than of a competing algorithm.

We then establish the boundary of that result, and in doing so correct a widely used
benchmark protocol. Over $29{,}760$ matched runs on twelve nonlinearly constrained problems
with five repair operators spanning distinct displacement geometries, the term does not
transfer: for each of the five operators it is significantly worse in more host-by-problem
cells than it is better, and all eight hosts are net harmed. A shifted-optimum control of
$46{,}080$ matched runs, in which each landscape appears twice and differs only in whether
its optimum sits at the domain centre, shows that the apparent benefit of the
radial-shrinkage operator, the geometry used by a widely adopted benchmark protocol, is a
centre-seeking artefact: it helps when the optimum lies at the domain centre and hurts
when the same landscape is translated away from it, with a median centre advantage of
$+0.368$ in $\log_{10}$ gap ratio at $D=30$. The natural explanation that a displacement
helps in proportion to the geometric information it carries is falsified alongside it: the
uninformative null control is the least harmful operator of the five, while the ellipsoid
projection, whose displacement is a true surface normal with $D$ degrees of freedom, loses
the largest share of matched pairs and is the only operator significantly harmful whether
or not the optimum sits at the domain centre.

We could not reproduce the working regime by imitating its surface features: the loop's
repair geometry, limit magnitude, budget, dimensionality, objective rank-deficiency, plant
lag and long-traverse initialisation, singly and in combination, never made the term
beneficial, and a further campaign of $19{,}200$ runs in which the optimum drifts at four
rates refutes the tracking conjecture in the direction opposite to the one predicted, the
term becoming more harmful as the target moves faster. One quantitative signal does
survive and points at a mechanism: across the $\beta$ sweep the benefit tracks repair
saturation almost exactly, at Spearman $-0.964$ between distance AUC and repair rate
($p = 4.5 \times 10^{-4}$), so whatever the term supplies is delivered in proportion to how
often the rate limit engages. We report that regularity without committing to a direction
of action: establishing one requires fixing the sign convention of the applied
displacement in the deployed controller, which we have not done.

We conclude that displacement reuse is a control signal for rate-constrained tracking
rather than a general search signal; that benchmark results for this family should be read
as confounded unless a shifted-optimum control is reported alongside them; and that the
co-variation of benefit with repair saturation is the most promising remaining lead on the
dissociation.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.} algorithm components; repair operators; constraint handling;
within-host ablation; benchmark bias; centre-bias; differential evolution; grey wolf

\section{Introduction}

The optimisation literature has accumulated a large number of algorithms whose novelty is
metaphorical rather than structural, and a sustained critique has argued that the unit of
analysis should be the \emph{component} rather than the named
algorithm~\citep{sorensen2015,camacho2022}. A component contribution is specified as a
term in an update rule, evaluated by ablation inside hosts that are otherwise unchanged,
and reported with the conditions under which it helps and the conditions under which it
does not.

This paper applies that standard to one specific idea. When a candidate solution violates
a constraint or leaves the search domain, a repair operator moves it to an admissible
point. The vector between the proposed point and the repaired point, which we write
$\Delta r$, is ordinarily discarded. It is tempting to suppose that it is informative:
geometrically it points out of the violated region, so a search that remembers it might
avoid re-proposing the same violation, or might infer something about where the
admissible region lies. Mechanisms of this kind recur in the constrained-optimisation
literature under various names.

We test the idea directly, and the answer is negative in a way that is more informative
than a simple absence of effect. The mechanism is not merely neutral; it is harmful, and
the one setting in which a benefit has been reported for an operator of this family turns
out to be explained by a property of the benchmark rather than by the mechanism.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A component specification of displacement reuse with an exact zero-term baseline:
  at $\beta = 0$ the arm consumes no random numbers and is stream-identical to the
  unmodified host, verified for all eight hosts by an automated test.
\item A multi-geometry ablation over eight hosts, five repair operators and twelve
  constrained problems (29{,}760 matched runs), showing the term is net harmful under
  every geometry tested, including a null operator whose displacement is uninformative
  by construction.
\item A shifted-optimum control (46{,}080 matched runs) that measures, rather than
  argues, the centre-pull confound: the same landscape is optimised twice, differing only
  in whether the optimum sits at the domain centre.
\item A falsification of the obvious mechanistic explanation. If displacement reuse
  worked because the displacement carries geometric information, the operator carrying
  the most information would help the most. It harms the most.
\item A moving-target control (19{,}200 matched runs) that tests, and refutes, the
  remaining conjecture that the mechanism belongs to tracking rather than to static
  search. The static case is retained as an amplitude-zero arm inside the same grid, so
  the comparison is internal.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The component}

Let a host algorithm propose, for individual $i$, a candidate by its own update rule
$\mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}$. The component adds exactly one term:
\begin{equation}
  x_i \;=\; \mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}(\cdot) \;+\; \beta\,\Delta r_i ,
  \qquad
  \Delta r_i \;=\; x^{\text{used}}_i - x^{\text{stored}}_i ,
  \label{eq:component}
\end{equation}
where $x^{\text{stored}}_i$ is the point held in the population and $x^{\text{used}}_i$ is
the point actually evaluated after repair. For DE/rand/1/bin the complete rule reads
\begin{equation}
  x_i \;=\; x_{r_1} + F\,(x_{r_2} - x_{r_3}) \;+\; \beta\,\Delta r_i ,
\end{equation}
which differs from the canonical rule~\citep{storn1997} by the final term alone, so a
reader can diff the two in one line.

We name this term CREO, for \emph{Continuous Repair Evolutionary Optimiser}, so that it
can be cited and built on. The name denotes the term in Equation~\ref{eq:component} and
nothing else: it is not an algorithm, and a host carrying it is a \emph{configuration} of
that host rather than a new method. Earlier work by the author used the expansion
``Repair-Guided Evolutionary Optimisation'' for the same term; the present expansion is
adopted here because it matches the acronym, and the two refer to the identical
mechanism. We write ``DE with
CREO'' or ``$\beta > 0$'' in preference to constructions such as ``CREO-DE'', because a
roster of hyphenated names implies a family of algorithms where there is one coefficient.

Two properties make this an ablation rather than a comparison of algorithms. First, the
term is additive and draws no random numbers, so setting $\beta = 0$ recovers the
unmodified host \emph{exactly}, including its random stream; this is asserted for all
eight hosts by a test that forces $\Delta r \equiv 0$ and requires bit-identical
trajectories. Second, the repair operator is a property of the environment and is applied
identically in both arms, so a matched pair of runs differs by the value of $\beta$ and
by nothing else. We hold $\beta = 0.90$ throughout and do not tune it; unbalanced tuning
effort between a proposed component and its baseline is a known failure mode, and leaving
the coefficient fixed is the conservative choice in a study whose finding is negative.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:component} states the component in full, inside a generic host loop.
The only line that differs from the unmodified host is line~\ref{line:term}; every other
line, including all random draws, is the host's own. Setting $\beta = 0$ therefore
recovers the host exactly rather than approximately, which is what licenses the matched
pairing used throughout.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Displacement reuse as a host-agnostic component. $\mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}$ is
the host's unmodified proposal rule; $\mathcal{R}$ is the repair operator; $\prec$ is the
host's own feasibility-aware comparison. The quantity $\Delta r$ is the only state the
component adds, one vector per individual.}
\label{alg:component}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require host $\mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}$, repair operator $\mathcal{R}$, coefficient
  $\beta$, budget $B$, problem $(f, g, \text{lo}, \text{hi})$
\State initialise population $P = \{x_1,\dots,x_N\}$ uniformly in $[\text{lo},\text{hi}]$
\For{$i = 1 \dots N$}
  \State $\Delta r_i \gets \mathbf{0}$
      \Comment{no predecessor exists at initialisation, so no repair is applied}
\EndFor
\While{evaluations used $< B$}
  \For{each individual $i$}
    \State $\tilde{x} \gets \mathcal{U}_{\text{host}}(P, i)$
        \Comment{host proposal, consuming the host's random draws}
    \State $\tilde{x} \gets \tilde{x} + \beta\,\Delta r_i$
        \label{line:term}
        \Comment{\textbf{the component: one added term, no random draws}}
    \State $x^{\text{stored}} \gets \mathrm{clip}(\tilde{x}, \text{lo}, \text{hi})$
    \State $x^{\text{used}} \gets \mathrm{clip}\big(\mathcal{R}(x^{\text{stored}},
           x^{\text{prev}}_i), \text{lo}, \text{hi}\big)$
        \Comment{repair; identical in both arms}
    \State $\Delta r_i \gets x^{\text{used}} - x^{\text{stored}}$
        \Comment{retain the displacement for the next iteration}
    \State evaluate $f(x^{\text{used}})$ and violation $v(x^{\text{used}})$
    \State $c \gets (x^{\text{stored}}, x^{\text{used}}, \Delta r_i, f, v)$
    \If{$c \prec P_i$}
      \State $P_i \gets c$
        \Comment{host selection, unchanged}
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndWhile
\State \Return best individual by $\prec$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Three details in Algorithm~\ref{alg:component} are load-bearing and are easy to get wrong.
First, the displacement is measured against the \emph{stored} point, not the proposal, so
that domain clipping is attributed to the repair operator rather than to the component.
Second, $\Delta r_i$ is read at the start of an iteration and written at the end, so the
term always uses the previous iteration's displacement; using the current one would make
the update implicit. Third, the operator receives $x^{\text{prev}}_i$, the incumbent the
candidate was generated from. Omitting that argument makes a rate-limiting operator a
silent no-op, because a rate limit is undefined without a predecessor: the arm then
returns ties for every run and reads as a null result rather than as a bug. We check for
this explicitly before each campaign.

\section{Repair operators}
\label{sec:method}

The central question is whether the usefulness of $\Delta r$ depends on the
\emph{geometry} of the operator that produced it. Five operators are therefore used,
spanning distinct displacement geometries, each parameterised on the search box:

\begin{description}
\item[Radial shrinkage.] Contracts the candidate towards the domain centre outside a soft
  region. Its displacement has one degree of freedom, its magnitude, and always points at
  the centre. This is the geometry used by the preferred-region protocol of a widely
  adopted benchmark suite.
\item[Box projection.] Componentwise projection onto a soft box. The displacement is
  axis-aligned and encodes only which coordinates were violated.
\item[Ellipsoid projection.] Projection onto a randomly oriented ellipsoid, fixed per
  problem so the geometry is part of the problem rather than part of the run. The
  displacement is a true surface normal with $D$ degrees of freedom, and is therefore the
  most geometrically informative operator in the battery.
\item[Rate limit.] Bounds each coordinate's change from the individual's predecessor, so
  the displacement is exactly the component of the requested step that the limiter
  refused. Of the five, this is the operator whose displacement most directly encodes a
  direction the search \emph{wanted} to move.
\item[Random resample.] A null control: a violating candidate is replaced by a fresh
  uniform sample, so the displacement is uninformative by construction. Any benefit here
  is attributable to perturbation magnitude alone, not to guidance.
\end{description}

The rate limit requires a predecessor. We use the incumbent each candidate is generated
from: the target vector for the differential-evolution variants and for the grey-wolf and
$\varepsilon$-constrained hosts, the child's own parent for the genetic algorithm, and the
particle's current position for particle swarm. CMA-ES has no per-individual predecessor,
so we use the distribution mean; we flag this as an analogy rather than a true
predecessor. The initial population is left unrepaired, since sampling has no
predecessor.

\section{Hosts and problems}

Eight hosts span algorithmic-component space rather than metaphor space: DE/rand/1/bin
and DE/current-to-$p$best/1~\citep{storn1997}, L-SHADE~\citep{tanabe2014},
SPSO-2011~\citep{zambrano2013}, a real-coded genetic algorithm with simulated binary
crossover and polynomial mutation, the Grey Wolf Optimiser~\citep{mirjalili2014},
$\varepsilon$-constrained differential evolution~\citep{takahama2009}, and
CMA-ES~\citep{hansen2001}. The Grey Wolf Optimiser is included because it is widely used
in the applied literature, with the explicit acknowledgement that its update rule has been
shown to be a reiteration of particle-swarm ideas rather than a novel
mechanism~\citep{camacho2022}; it appears here as a host, not as a contribution.
Selection is feasibility-aware in every host, following Deb's rules~\citep{deb2000}, so
that feasibility handling is identical across arms.

The constrained campaign uses twelve nonlinearly constrained problems from the standard
G-suite together with two engineering design problems, as implemented in
\texttt{pymoo}~\citep{blank2020}. The shifted-optimum control uses the 24 BBOB
functions~\citep{hansen2021} through the \texttt{IOHexperimenter}
interface~\citep{denobel2023}. All runs use a budget of $2\times10^4$ function
evaluations.

\section{Experimental design}

\paragraph{Constrained campaign.} Eight hosts $\times$ five operators $\times$ twelve
problems $\times$ 31 paired seeds $\times$ two arms gives 29{,}760 runs. The repair
operator acts on the search domain; the problems' own nonlinear constraints are handled
by feasibility-aware selection, identically in both arms.

\paragraph{Shifted-optimum control.} This is the decisive test of the centre-pull
confound. Each BBOB function, at instances 1--5 and $D \in \{10,30\}$, is run in two
variants that differ \emph{only} in where the optimum sits: \emph{translated}, $f(x)$,
with the optimum at $x^\star$ away from the domain centre, and \emph{centred},
$f(x+x^\star)$, the same landscape with the optimum at the origin, which is the centre of
the box. Three operators $\times$ two seeds $\times$ two arms gives 46{,}080 runs. An
operator whose displacement points at the domain centre must help in the centred variant
and not in the translated one. If it does, a benefit reported on a suite whose optima are
biased towards the centre is a centre-seeking artefact rather than repair
guidance~\citep{kudela2022}.

\paragraph{Moving-target control.} The static campaigns leave one conjecture open: that
displacement reuse belongs to \emph{tracking} rather than to repair geometry. We test it
inside a single design. Each of six BBOB functions, spanning separable to highly
multimodal, is optimised at $D \in \{10,30\}$ with its optimum displaced along a
deterministic trajectory
\begin{equation}
  s(t) \;=\; A\,\tfrac{1}{2}(\text{hi}-\text{lo})\,\sin\!\big(2\pi t / T\big)\,\hat{d},
  \qquad T = B/4,
\end{equation}
where $t$ is the evaluation index, $\hat{d}$ a unit direction fixed per function, and $A$
the drift amplitude as a fraction of the domain half-width. Crucially $A = 0$ reproduces
the static case, so the control is \emph{internal} to the campaign rather than a
comparison across campaigns. Eight hosts $\times$ five operators $\times$ six functions
$\times$ two dimensions $\times$ four amplitudes $\times$ five seeds $\times$ two arms
gives 19{,}200 runs. Because the schedule depends on $t$ alone it is identical in both
arms of a matched pair. Best-so-far objective value is meaningless when the optimum
moves, so the reported measure is \emph{tracking error}: the mean distance from each
evaluated point to the optimum's current location, normalised by the domain width.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Arms are paired exactly on host, operator, problem, dimension and
seed. On the constrained suite, runs are ordered by the feasibility-aware key, matching
the selection rule used inside the hosts; the objective is compared by Wilcoxon
signed-rank over the 31 seeds within each host-by-operator-by-problem cell, restricted to
runs feasible in both arms, and cells with fewer than six such pairs are reported as
untested rather than silently dropped. On the shifted-optimum control the paired quantity
is $\log_{10}$ of the ratio of optimality gaps, positive favouring the component, and the
confound is tested by comparing the centred and translated improvements for the same
landscape, which are themselves a matched pair. All $p$-values are Holm-corrected within
their family~\citep{holm1979}.

Two observations bear on the mechanism. The null operator, whose displacement is a fresh
uniform sample and therefore carries no guidance at all, is not the worst of the five: it

is the mildest.

\subsection{The tracking conjecture, tested and refuted}

If the mechanism's premise is that a refused step describes a constraint the search is
actively fighting, then its value should rise as the target moves. It does not.
Table~\ref{tab:drift} reports the effect at each drift amplitude: at every level between
five and six of the ten operator-by-dimension cells are significantly harmful and
\emph{none} is significantly helpful, and the median benefit becomes more negative as the
target moves faster, from $-0.0028$ static to $-0.0046$ at $A = 0.20$.

\section{A deployed closed loop, where the term does work}
\label{sec:loop}

Every result so far is negative. We now report the one environment in which the term
demonstrably helps, because the contrast is the most informative part of this study and
because a mechanism paper that reported only the negative half would be as selective as
one reporting only the positive half.

The environment is a rate-constrained set-point tracking loop: a controller proposes a
six-dimensional bounded control each cycle, a rate limiter clips it to
$\pm 0.10$ of range about the previously applied control, a two-dimensional plant with
first-order lag responds, and the score is the distance of the plant state from a target
integrated over fifteen cycles, at twenty evaluations per cycle, giving a 300-evaluation
budget. The repair operator is therefore exactly the rate-limit geometry of
Section~\ref{sec:method}, and the displacement is exactly the refused component of the
requested control change. Each arm is run over 67{,}200 matched episodes across 21 stress
profiles.

\subsection{An isolated dose-response in $\beta$}

The decisive measurement is a sweep of $\beta$ within one controller, holding every other
term fixed. In this implementation the component block executes whenever repair guidance
is enabled, irrespective of the value of $\beta$, so the $\beta = 0$ arm consumes the
identical random stream: it is an exact zero-term baseline of the kind
Section~\ref{sec:method} requires. Table~\ref{tab:dose} reports the result.

Distance AUC decreases strictly monotonically across all seven levels and success rises
with it, from $0.578$ at $\beta = 0$ to $0.659$ at $\beta = 1.50$, and the effect has not
saturated at the largest value tested. Decomposing the gain of the deployed configuration
over the bare host on this condition: the total improvement in AUC is $3.72\%$, of which
$0.84\%$ is attributable to the other differences between the two arms and $2.91\%$ to
$\beta$ alone, so the displacement term accounts for $78.1\%$ of the effect. On this
environment the mechanism works, it works through the term under study, and the evidence
is a dose-response rather than a single comparison.

\paragraph{The benefit tracks repair saturation.} The same sweep carries a signal about
mechanism. The fraction of evaluations on which the rate limit actually fires rises
monotonically with $\beta$, from $0.8770$ at $\beta = 0$ to $0.9999$ at $\beta = 1.50$, and
across the seven levels distance AUC and that firing rate are almost perfectly
anti-correlated (Spearman $-0.964$, $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-4}$, $n = 7$). The component is
therefore not simply improving the loop; it is improving it in lockstep with how often the
rate limit engages. We record the regularity and stop short of a mechanism. Two readings
are available and they point in opposite directions: $\Delta r$ as defined is the
correction the limiter applied, which is directed inward, so re-injecting it damps the
step; but the firing rate rises with $\beta$, which is what one would expect if candidates
were being driven further outside the admissible box. Deciding between these requires
fixing the sign convention of the applied displacement in the deployed controller against
its implementation, which is outside the scope of this paper. We note the test that would
settle it: whether a policy that always moves at the maximum admissible rate towards the
set point reproduces the component's advantage. If it does, the effect reduces to
saturation; if it does not, the term supplies something a saturating policy cannot.

\subsection{What the ranking does and does not say}

Table~\ref{tab:rank} gives the mean rank of twelve arms over the 21 profiles. The term
improves every host it is attached to, in every one of the 21 profiles, by between

and the relative gain is strongly inversely related to host quality (Spearman $+0.83$
between a host's own AUC and the gain the term delivers on it). The correct reading is
that the term improves its host, that it improves a weak host more than a strong one, and
that host selection still dominates component selection.

\subsection{An unexplained dissociation}

The term helps here and harms everywhere else in this paper, and we were unable to find
the reason. We reproduced, one at a time and in combination, every structural feature of
this loop we could identify: the rate-limit geometry and its exact limit magnitude, the
300-evaluation budget, the six-dimensional bounded control space, a rank-deficient
objective matching the loop's six-to-two map, the first-order plant lag, a moving target
at four rates, and a plant initialised on the opposite side of its target so that each
episode begins with a long rate-limited traverse. In none of these did the term become
beneficial; the rank-deficient, rate-limited, lagged condition that most closely matches
the loop is in fact the most harmful cell in the entire study (median benefit $-0.0157$,
helped in $23.9\%$ of matched pairs).

One structural difference remains, which we name but did not test. In the loop the rate
limit is applied about a \emph{single} applied control that advances once per cycle, so the
whole population is confined to one moving box and every individual's displacement is the
refused part of a step from a \emph{shared} operating point. In our replications each
individual is limited about its own predecessor, so the accumulated displacements point in
as many directions as there are individuals. That difference turns $\Delta r$ from a
per-individual quantity into a population-coherent one, and it is the candidate we would
test next.

Two candidate explanations therefore stand, and they are not exclusive: population
coherence, described above, and the saturation account of
Section~\ref{sec:loop}, under which the term works by driving the control against its rate
limit rather than by indicating a direction. Neither is settled, and until one of them is, the honest summary is bounded: the term is a usable
signal in at least one deployed rate-constrained tracking loop, where its effect is
isolated by a monotone dose-response against a stream-identical baseline; it does not
transfer to static benchmark problems under any of five repair geometries; and the
structural property that separates the two regimes has been narrowed to two candidates
rather than identified.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What is falsified.} The intuition motivating displacement reuse is that
$\Delta r$ carries directional information about the admissible region. If that were the
operative mechanism, benefit should increase with the information content of the
displacement. It does not. The ordering is not a clean reversal, and we do not claim one: by
significantly-harmful cells the single-degree-of-freedom radial shrinkage is worst, while
by share of matched pairs lost the $D$-degree-of-freedom ellipsoid projection is. What the
data do show is that information does not buy benefit. The null operator, whose
displacement is pure noise by construction, is the \emph{least} harmful of the five on
both measures, losing $37.5\%$ of pairs against the ellipsoid's $81.6\%$; and the
ellipsoid is the only operator significantly harmful in both variants of the
shifted-optimum control. Whatever the term is doing, it is not conveying constraint
geometry to the search.

\paragraph{What the benchmark result was.} Where a benefit for a radial-shrinkage-style
operator has been reported on a standard suite, the shifted-optimum control explains it
without reference to repair at all. The displacement points at the domain centre; on a
suite whose optima are disproportionately near the centre, a centre pull is a free gain,
and the same pull is a penalty once the optimum is moved. This stacks with the known
centre-bias of several widely used benchmark suites~\citep{kudela2022}: a mechanism that
happens to bias search towards the origin will appear to work, and will stop working on
any instance set that does not share that bias. We would suggest that any mechanism whose
displacement has a systematic direction in the search box should report a shifted-optimum
control as a matter of course.

\paragraph{What remains open.} The three defences of the mechanism \emph{as a
constraint-geometry device} have been tested and have failed: that the displacement
carries useful geometry (falsified by the ellipsoid operator being the most harmful), that
the reported benchmark benefit reflects repair guidance (explained instead by
domain-centre bias), and that it belongs to tracking rather than static search (refuted,
with harm increasing as the target moves faster).

What survives is harder and more interesting. Section~\ref{sec:loop} establishes, with a
monotone dose-response against an exact zero-term baseline, that the term does real work
in one deployed closed loop. Seven structural features of that loop, replicated on standard problems as eight
conditions, all failed to make it beneficial. So we are left with a mechanism that
is reliably harmful in every controlled setting we could construct and reliably helpful in
the uncontrolled one it was designed for, and we cannot yet say why. We name the
population-coherence of the displacement as the leading candidate: a rate limit about a
single shared operating point makes every individual's $\Delta r$ a component of the same
refused step, whereas a per-individual limit makes them point in unrelated directions.

We therefore do not claim that no instance of this family can help. We claim that it does
not help for the reasons usually given, that two of the benefits previously reported for it
are confounded, and that the one benefit we can confirm is not reproduced by any
controlled variation we attempted.

\section{Threats to validity}

The coefficient $\beta$ is fixed at $0.90$ and untuned; a tuned coefficient might reduce
the harm, though it is hard to see how tuning recovers a benefit from a term whose
uninformative variant is the least harmful of the five. A single evaluation budget is used, and the
dimensional trend visible in the shifted-optimum control suggests budget and dimension
interact. The predecessor mapping required by the rate-limit operator is a design choice,
and the CMA-ES mapping in particular is an analogy; a different mapping might change that
operator's behaviour, although it is already the mildest of the five. A substantial
number of constrained cells are untested because too few seed pairs were feasible in both
arms, between 21 and 47 of 96 depending on operator, so the constrained result speaks to
the problems where feasibility was attainable within budget rather than to the suite as a
whole. Finally, seven of the eight hosts reproduce their repair fire rates bit-identically
across the two platforms we ran on, but CMA-ES does not, because the reference
implementation differs in version; that arm should be reproduced only against a pinned
version. The closed-loop result carries a separate caveat: the benefit reported in
Section~\ref{sec:loop} has been established within the deployed system and its dose-response
is internal to it, but we have not reproduced that benefit in a harness rebuilt from the
loop's stated parameters alone. Those parameters therefore do not appear to be sufficient
to specify the regime, which is consistent with the dissociation remaining unexplained and
is a reason to treat the single-environment scope of that result as a real limitation
rather than a formality.
